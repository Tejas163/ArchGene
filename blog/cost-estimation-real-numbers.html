LLM Cost Estimation: Real Numbers

GPU costs aren't abstract. They're specific, calculable numbers that determine whether your project is feasible or fantasy. Here's the math.

The Basic Formula

Training cost breaks down to:

Cost = (GPU hours) × (GPU cost per hour)

GPU hours depends on your model size, token count, and hardware. Let's run through it.

Model Parameters

For a decoder-only transformer, parameter count is approximately:

P ≈ 12 × L × D²

Where L = layers, D = hidden dimension. (Ignoring embedding and output heads for now—they're usually small compared to transformer blocks.)

Training FLOPs

For training (forward + backward pass):

FLOPs ≈ 6 × P × T

Where T = training tokens. This is per forward pass. Backward pass is ~2x forward. So total ≈ 6× forward.

Example: Llama-2 7B trained on 2 trillion tokens.
FLOPs ≈ 6 × 6.7B × 2T ≈ 80 zettaFLOPs

Real Cost Examples

GPT-2 Medium (345M) on 1B tokens

  • FLOPs: 6 × 345M × 1B = 2.07 × 10¹⁵
  • A100-40GB hours: 2.07 × 10¹⁵ / 312 × 10¹² = 6.6 hours
  • Cost (spot): ~$7
  • Cost (on-demand): ~$22

Llama-2 7B on 100B tokens

  • FLOPs: 6 × 6.7B × 100B = 4 × 10²¹
  • A100-40GB hours: 4 × 10²¹ / 312 × 10¹² = 12,820 hours
  • With 64 GPUs: 200 hours ≈ 8.3 days
  • Cost (64 A100 spot): ~$14,000
  • Cost (64 A100 on-demand): ~$44,000

Llama-2 70B on 2T tokens

  • FLOPs: 6 × 68.9B × 2T = 8.3 × 10²³
  • A100-40GB hours: 8.3 × 10²³ / 312 × 10¹² = 2,660,000 hours
  • With 512 GPUs: 5,195 hours ≈ 216 days
  • Cost (512 A100 spot): ~$1.4M
  • Cost (512 A100 on-demand): ~$4.4M

The Scaling Sweet Spot

For most teams, aim for:

  • 7B-13B models for fine-tuning and experimentation
  • 100B-1T tokens for pre-training from scratch
  • 64-128 A100s for realistic training timelines

Architecture mistakes at 7B scale cost $10K-$50K. At 70B, they cost $1M+.

The Point

Cost estimation isn't about being precise. It's about being in the right order of magnitude. If someone tells you training their 70B model will cost "$10K", that's a red flag. If they say "$500K-$1M", that's realistic.

Before you commit to an architecture, run the numbers. If the number scares you, consider a smaller model, fewer tokens, or a different architecture.
