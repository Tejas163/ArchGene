Why Formal Verification Matters for LLMs

Most teams treat architecture design as intuition. "This feels right." "Everyone uses something similar." "Let's just try and see."

That's a $50,000 mistake waiting to happen.

The Intuition Trap

Here's what I see repeatedly: a team decides to train a 70B parameter model. They pick hidden_dim=8192, heads=64, layers=80. Looks reasonable. Resembles Llama, Mistral, other successful models.

They spend 3 months collecting 500B tokens of training data. They provision 128 A100s. Training starts. Three weeks in, they notice something wrong.

"We're getting NaN losses at layer 23. It happens sporadically. We tried lowering learning rate, adding gradient clipping. Still happens."

The architecture has a hidden incompatibility that only manifests after significant training. By then, you've wasted a month on compute, and the team is demoralized.

What Formal Verification Catches

ArchGene uses Z3 theorem proving to verify your architecture before you spend a dollar on compute. It checks constraints like:

  • Hidden dimension divisibility: hidden_dim must be divisible by (num_heads * head_dim)
  • Attention compatibility: attention mechanism must be compatible with your hidden_dim
  • Position encoding limits: max_position must fit in your encoding type
  • FFN constraints: intermediate_dim relationships that GELU expects

The fintech startup case? We caught the hidden_dim (8192) was incompatible with their target GPU cluster before they spent $1 on training. Saved $85K in expected compute waste.

The Cost Math

A single A100-80GB costs ~$3/hour on spot instances. A 70B model training run might need 64-128 GPUs for 2-4 weeks. That's $30,000-$100,000+ per training attempt.

Formal verification costs $500 for a Solo package. The math is absurd in your favor.

When Intuition Makes Sense

Intuition is valuable when:

  • You've trained 10+ models and seen what works
  • You're iterating on a known-working architecture
  • You have the budget to experiment

Intuition is dangerous when:

  • Your first LLM architecture
  • Your training budget is fixed
  • Your team has no LLM-specific experience

The Point

Formal verification isn't about being paranoid. It's about being smart. Before you commit $50K+ in compute, spend $500 to know it will work.

The alternative is intuiting your way to a $50K mistake.
