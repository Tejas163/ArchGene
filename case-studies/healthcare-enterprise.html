Enterprise Fine-tuning Strategy

A healthcare enterprise with 1,000+ employees was planning to fine-tune language models across 5 different use cases: clinical documentation, patient intake, drug interaction checking, billing codes, and medical imaging reports.

Each of their 5 teams had independently designed their own fine-tuning approach. When the AI infrastructure team reviewed the total requirements, they discovered the combined compute needs were unsustainable with their existing GPU budget.

The Situation

Five teams, five different architectures:

  • Clinical Documentation: Llama-2 70B (infrastructure: 256 A100s)
  • Patient Intake: Mistral 7B (infrastructure: 8 A100s)
  • Drug Interactions: Custom 40B (infrastructure: 64 A100s)
  • Billing Codes: Llama-2 13B (infrastructure: 16 A100s)
  • Medical Imaging: Custom 70B (infrastructure: 128 A100s)

Total required: 472 A100s running 24/7. Annual infrastructure cost: $1.2M.

Available budget: $850K. Deficit: $350K.

What ArchGene Found

Finding #1: Architecture Overkill

Three of the five use cases (Patient Intake, Billing Codes, Drug Interactions) didn't require their planned model sizes. A 7B model was sufficient for two of them; a 13B was sufficient for Drug Interactions. The teams had oversized "to be safe."

Finding #2: Shared Infrastructure Possible

The teams hadn't considered shared infrastructure. With proper job scheduling and the right architecture choices, they could consolidate to 128 A100s with job queuing instead of dedicated clusters.

Finding #3: Architecture Duplication

Two teams had independently designed architectures that violated the same constraint. They were both spending engineering time debugging the same root cause.

The Recommendations

Consolidated recommendation:

  • 7B model for Patient Intake and Billing Codes (shared cluster: 16 A100s)
  • 13B model for Drug Interactions
  • 70B model for Clinical Documentation and Medical Imaging (with shared cluster: 96 A100s)

The Outcome

Revised infrastructure: 128 A100s with job queuing. Annual cost: $680K. Savings: $520K upfront, $120K annually versus original projections.

"We thought we needed 5 different architectures because we had 5 different use cases. ArchGene showed us that architecture and use case are different problems. Same architecture can serve different use cases with different fine-tuning."

Beyond cost savings, they gained:

  • Unified evaluation pipeline across all teams
  • Shared tooling and debugging infrastructure
  • Cross-team learning and best practice sharing

What They Learned

1. Fine-tuning doesn't require unique architectures per use case. One architecture, multiple fine-tuned checkpoints.

2. Team autonomy in architecture choice leads to infrastructure inefficiency at the org level.

3. Standardization enables shared learning without sacrificing specialization.
